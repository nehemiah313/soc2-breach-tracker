Why companies pursue SOC 2

A SOC 2 report gives customers independent assurance that a service provider's controls are designed well and operating effectively. Enterprise buyers increasingly require one before they will trust a vendor with their data, so SOC 2 often becomes the key that unlocks larger deals. It also forces the discipline that prevents the incidents below: access controls, monitoring, incident response, vendor oversight, and honest breach communication.

The incidents listed here are real, publicly reported events. The figures are reported costs, settlements, fines, or ransom payments from the cited sources, shown so teams can see what weak controls actually cost. They are not predictions, and every incident is different.

About the SOC 2 criteria tags

Each incident is tagged with the Trust Services Criteria that most closely relate to the control failure described in public reporting. These tags are illustrative, not findings: they show which criteria a SOC 2 engagement would have examined, so teams can see how readiness work maps to real incidents. Criterion titles come from the TSC dataset (61 criteria).
